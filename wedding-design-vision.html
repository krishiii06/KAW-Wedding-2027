Mangalsnanam / Nalugu

Rhythm Events Space, garden · Outdoor
Guests120–150
TimeMorning
ReferenceSobhita Dhulipala's Nalugu

Nalugu is our version of Haldi. Family and friends apply a turmeric paste on us as a blessing, and it's playful and messy in the best way. It's followed by the Mangalsnanam, a ceremonial bath that marks the start of the wedding.

It's in the garden, traditional and warm, and very close in spirit to Sobhita Dhulipala's Nalugu. I'll send more inspiration for this one.

Same day as the Carnival. The garden setup and the Carnival lawn will be going up at the same time, so the two designs should be planned together.

Carnival

Rhythm Events Space, main lawn · Outdoor
Guests~300
Starts4pm, into the night
ReplacesMehendi + Sangeeth

The one I'm most excited about! Instead of a Mehendi and Sangeeth, we're doing a carnival inspired by the Godavari region of Andhra, known for its river villages, coconut groves, crafts, food and village fairs. It should work for every age, from our parents to our friends, all in the same space.

What's in it
Art stalls

Godavari-inspired stalls with traditional art and crafts.

Games + activities

Fun things everyone can join in on, no performing needed.

Gorintaku

Gorintaku is henna the old way: fresh leaves ground by hand, applied in simple traditional patterns. It replaces a regular mehendi setup.

This has the most moving parts. Several zones in one lawn, a daytime-to-night change in light, and a lot of people moving between stalls. The layout and flow matter as much as the look.
